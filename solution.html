The solution

A simple geometric change. A complete fix.

We didn't reinvent the mushroom anchor — we removed its flaw. Three symmetrically positioned bars turn the stem into a cone the chain can't wrap. It still beds into mud, still gains suction, still holds.

The problem

Every mushroom anchor in a soft-bottom harbor has the same flaw.

The bowl sinks into mud and gains suction over time — but its vertical stem is the weakness. As tides, currents, and wind swing the boat, the chain rotates around the stem. Once it catches, every cycle pulls it tighter.

Effective scope shrinks, peak loads rise
Mooring balls pull under at high tide
Chain or shackle failure under load
Boats break free — the harbormaster gets the call

Three bars. One geometry.

The bars rise from the bowl to a single point above the stem. The result is a cone — and a cone has nowhere for chain to catch.
