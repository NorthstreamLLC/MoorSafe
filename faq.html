Questions, answered.

What harbormasters, mooring services and boat owners ask before they order a MoorSafe. Don't see yours? Ask us.

How it works

What is chain wrap, and why does it matter?

On a traditional mushroom anchor, the stem is a vertical post. As tides, currents and wind swing a moored boat through 360°, the chain rotates around the stem. Once it catches, every cycle pulls it tighter.

The effective scope shrinks and peak loads rise. Mooring balls can pull under at high tide, chain or shackles can fail under load, and boats can break free. That is the call a harbormaster gets. Read the full explanation.

How does the MoorSafe anchor stop chain wrap?

Three symmetrically positioned bars rise from the bowl to a single point above the stem, forming a cone. The chain has nothing to catch on, so it slides off as the boat swings, which significantly reduces the risk of chain wrap.

It is a simple geometric change to the mushroom anchor: the flaw is removed, and the rest of the design stays as it was. See how it works.

Does adding the bars reduce holding power?

The bowl is the same concave mushroom bowl. It still beds into the mud and gains suction over time.

In the Casco Bay field test, both anchors sat on the same muddy seabed for 28 days and were then pulled by a lobster boat on a 75-ft chain at 3:1 scope, with tension read on a 4,000 lb digital indicator. The traditional anchor, partially buried, broke out at 1,575 lb. MoorSafe, fully buried, broke out at 1,295 lb. That is comparable breakout, and MoorSafe was fully buried, so its holding keeps improving as sediment consolidates. Read the field test write-up.

Is it as strong as a regular mushroom anchor?

In tensile testing at Yale Cordage in Saco, Maine, a 300 lb MoorSafe prototype held to 40,657 lb, against 38,560 lb for a 300 lb traditional mushroom. That is roughly 5% higher.

The traditional anchor failed by fracturing at the stem. The MoorSafe prototype failed when its joints dislocated. The test ran on a 100-ft horizontal bed rated to 1.3 million lb. Read the tensile test write-up.

What is the anchor made of?

MoorSafe has a cast-iron base with welded steel bars, finished in high-visibility safety orange.

Will it work in my harbor?

MoorSafe is a mushroom anchor for soft-bottom harbors, the same use as the anchor it improves on. It was field-tested on a muddy seabed in Casco Bay. If you are unsure about your bottom or exposure, ask us and we will help you decide.

Sizing, quotes and ordering

Is the 400 lb right for my boat?

The 400 lb is recommended for boats of about 30 to 40 ft, or for heavier displacement and moderate exposure. Sizing depends on displacement, scope and exposure, so we confirm it with you before you order. Not sure? Tell us about your mooring and we will help you decide.

Do you offer a larger size?

The 400 lb is our current size. If you are looking for a larger anchor, send us a request and tell us about your mooring, including boat length, harbor and exposure.

How much does it cost?

Installation and use

Do I need different chain or hardware?

No. Use the same chain, shackles, swivel and pennant you use today. Attach at the top eye.

Who installs it?

MoorSafe installs exactly like the anchor it replaces: lower it with a mooring barge or service boat, at the same scope and with the same practice as a mushroom. Any mooring service provider will be familiar with the process, and we can recommend providers in your area. Ask us for a recommendation.

Does it need special inspection?

No. Inspect on your normal schedule. Holding grows as the bowl embeds, and you inspect it on the same schedule as any mooring.
